# Do marketing guardrail Skills actually catch anything Claude wouldn't?

An eval of four ways to check marketing copy with Claude before it's published: plain Claude, plain Claude with a generic checklist, a set of free guardrail Skills, and the Guardrails for Marketing Teams pack. Built and run in November 2026 on Claude Sonnet 5.5.

Every draft ran three times per setup, and there's a fourth setup that gives plain Claude a generic checklist as long as the Guardrails text, to test whether length alone does the work.

**Short answer:** on catching planted problems, none of the setups can be told apart from plain Claude. Plain Claude caught 98 of 102, a generic checklist of the same length got 101, the free Skills 100 and the Guardrails pack 102 (counting a problem as caught when it was caught in at least two of three runs). The best paired test, plain Claude against Guardrails, gives p = 0.12. On publish or don't-publish calls, Guardrails was best (95% right) and plain Claude close behind (94%), with no real difference between them. The one clear result is that the free Skills over-flag: Guardrails beat them on publish calls with p = 0.004. Most of what a Skill adds seems to come from spelling the checks out at all, which a generic checklist does nearly as well, at about two thirds of the cost.

## What was tested

**The drafts.** A fictional UK tea brand, Pebblebrook Tea Co., with a written brand policy (claims it can prove, words it never uses, how it labels ads). 24 clean drafts across landing pages, emails, search ads, product pages, creator posts and an affiliate review, using only claims the policy allows. Then variants with problems planted at exact, known positions:
- **Round 1, obvious (29 problems):** "The UK's best loose leaf tea", "Loved by 50,000 tea drinkers", "boosts your immunity", "carbon neutral", "only 40 tins are left", "In today's fast-paced world", a creator post with its "Ad" label removed.
- **Round 2, subtle (22 problems):** the review count quietly changed from 1,200 to 2,000; "noticeably smoother than supermarket tea bags"; "help you wind down and sleep better"; "packed with antioxidants"; "planet-friendly packaging"; "while stocks last"; "#ad" buried in the middle of seven hashtags; an affiliate link disclosed only in a footnote; "Brewed with care. Packed with love. Delivered with a smile."; "Was £12, now £8" with no price history.

**The setups**, each given the same task and the same policy:
1. **Plain Claude:** "You are a careful marketing editor. Check marketing copy before it is published."
2. **Free Skills:** Claims Guard, Slop Guard and Honest Review Rules from the free, CC BY marketing Skills.
3. **Guardrails pack:** the publish gate plus its claims, regulated claims, disclosure and house style checks. It's a free download from marketingprompthub.com/guardrails/.
4. **Plain Claude plus a generic checklist** (`baseline_long`): see "The length control" below.

**Two policy levels.** Every round ran twice: once with the full brand policy (which spells out banned phrases), and once with a bare one (who the brand is and the claims it can prove, nothing more). Most real teams have something closer to the bare one. So the 102 problems are 51 planted problems, each tested at both policy levels.

**How it ran.** Each draft went to Claude headless with no tools, no settings and no memory (`claude -p`), and the reply had to end with a JSON list of problems, quoting the draft's exact words, plus a publish or don't-publish decision. 2,304 runs in all: 192 drafts, four setups, three repeats.

**How it was scored.** All the rules are in `score.py` and covered by tests.
- A planted problem counts as caught when a flagged quote matches it word for word: either the quote contains the whole problem, or it's a long enough piece of it (at least two meaningful words, or 40% of the problem's words) that sits inside it or shares 60% of its words. A one-word quote like "tea" or "ad" doesn't count.
- A missing label counts as caught only when a missing disclosure flag points at it: it quotes the draft's opening words (which the task asks for) or mentions the ad, gift, affiliate link or disclosure.
- If two flags hit the same planted problem, the second is counted as a duplicate, not hidden and not a false alarm.
- Every other flag was ruled by hand as **valid** (a real problem my test set had missed), a **false alarm**, or **borderline**, with a reason for each, in `data/adjudication.json`.

I tightened the scoring after a review pointed out the first version could count a one-word quote as a catch. Rescoring the same runs with the stricter rules changed no catches, so the headline numbers stand.

## Results

Generated by `python3 score.py summary`, which writes `results/summary.md`. Each draft ran three times per setup. A planted problem counts as caught when it was caught in at least two of the three runs; the mean across runs is in brackets.

**Planted problems caught**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plain Claude | 29/29 | 28/29 | 21/22 | 20/22 | **98/102** (97.7) |
| Plain Claude + generic checklist | 29/29 | 28/29 | 22/22 | 22/22 | **101/102** (100.7) |
| Free Skills | 29/29 | 29/29 | 20/22 | 22/22 | **100/102** (100.0) |
| Guardrails pack | 29/29 | 29/29 | 22/22 | 22/22 | **102/102** (101.7) |

**Publish calls and false alarms** (every draft and repeat counted)

| | Publish call right | Clean drafts blocked | Extra flags: real problems I'd missed / false alarms / borderline | Cost per 50 drafts | Seconds per draft |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plain Claude | 539/576 (94%) | 24/288 (8%) | 43 / 8 / 0 | $0.41 | 10 |

| Free Skills | 530/576 (92%) | 40/288 (14%) | 39 / 44 / 28 | $0.60 | 13 |
| Guardrails pack | 550/576 (95%) | 25/288 (9%) | 38 / 17 / 10 | $0.63 | 13 |

A clean draft is one with nothing planted.

**Are the differences real?** Exact McNemar tests on the same 102 problems and 192 publish calls (majority of three runs). "A only" is how many A got right that B didn't.

| | Problems: A only, B only, p | Publish calls: A only, B only, p |
|---|---|---|
| Plain Claude vs Guardrails pack | 0, 4, p = 0.12 | 2, 6, p = 0.29 |
| Plain Claude vs generic checklist | 1, 4, p = 0.38 | 4, 4, p = 1.00 |
| Generic checklist vs Guardrails pack | 0, 1, p = 1.00 | 2, 6, p = 0.29 |
| Plain Claude vs Free Skills | 1, 3, p = 0.62 | 8, 3, p = 0.23 |
| Free Skills vs Guardrails pack | 0, 2, p = 0.50 | 0, 9, p = 0.004 |

The 102 problems are 51 problems at two policy levels, so a single weakness can count twice, which makes these p values if anything generous.

## What it means

- **On catching problems, it's a tie.** Every setup caught 98 or more of 102, and no setup is clearly better than plain Claude.
- **Plain Claude's misses repeat, so they're real.** It missed the tidy "Brewed with care. Packed with love. Delivered with a smile." triplet in five of six runs, the affiliate link disclosed only in a footnote in all three bare-policy runs, and a creator post with its "Gifted" label removed in two of three bare-policy runs. Guardrails caught all of them every time. So there is something there, just not enough problems to prove it statistically.
- **A generic checklist gets most of the way.** Plain Claude plus a long, generic review checklist caught 101 of 102 at two thirds of Guardrails' cost. Its one miss is odd: "Today only: 30% off, ends at midnight" slipped past it in all three bare-policy runs. Most of the value seems to be in spelling the checks out, not in any one wording of them.
- **Publish calls are close at the top.** Guardrails got 95% right and plain Claude 94%. Neither blocks many clean drafts (9% and 8%).
- **The free Skills over-flag.** They blocked 14% of clean drafts and raised the most false alarms (44), mostly by questioning claims the policy allows and asking for disclosure that was already there. Guardrails beat them on publish calls (p = 0.004). That's the first thing to fix in them.
- **The obvious stuff is solved.** Round 1 was near perfect for everyone. If your worry is "Claude will let 'clinically proven' through", it mostly won't.
- **The written policy mattered less than I expected.** Most misses came under the bare policy, but only by a problem or two.

## The length control

The plain Claude prompt is one line, and both Skill setups add thousands of words. So a fair question is whether the Skills help just because they make the prompt longer and more specific. The fourth setup, `baseline_long`, is plain Claude plus a generic marketing review checklist (`data/generic-review-checklist.md`) of about the same length as the Guardrails text (11,169 characters against 10,694). It's written from general advertising review common sense: claims need evidence, no health claims, label ads and affiliate links, no fake urgency, avoid AI clichés. It doesn't use the Guardrails text, and a test checks it shares no three-word phrase with any planted problem, so it isn't being taught the answers either.

## What I'd change next

1. **Make round 3 harder still,** with problems that need context, such as a claim that's true for one product but not another, or a disclosure that's present but not prominent enough on mobile. Write it before looking at any Skill text.
2. **Cut the free Skills' false alarms** by teaching them to check the policy's approved claims before flagging, then rerun.
3. **Test another model.** Everything here is Claude Sonnet 5.5; a smaller model might show bigger gaps between the setups.

## Limits, honestly

- I wrote the drafts, the planted problems and the rulings, and I also wrote the Skills being tested, both the free ones and Guardrails. The rulings and their reasons are all in the repo so you can disagree with them. Guardrails is now free, so I've no commercial interest in it winning, but I'd still rather it did, so read my rulings with that in mind.
- The round 2 cases were written after the Skills, by the person who wrote the Skills. That's a real risk of designing problems the Skills were already built to catch, which would flatter both Skill setups.
- The plain Claude prompt is a one-liner. `baseline_long` tests whether length alone explains the Skills, and it mostly does.
- Three runs per draft at default temperature. That's enough to tell a steady miss from a fluke, but not enough problems to separate setups that are this close.
- One fictional brand, UK English, one model.
- The 102 problems are 51 problems at two policy levels, so they aren't 102 independent tests.
- Asking for a JSON list of problems changes how the model answers a little. All setups had the same instruction.

## Run it yourself

```
python3 data/build_cases.py && python3 data/build_cases_v2.py
POLICY=minimal python3 run.py v1-min                          # round 1, bare policy
CASES=cases-v2.json POLICY=full python3 run.py v2-full        # round 2, full policy
REPEATS=3 CASES=cases-v2.json python3 run.py v2-full baseline # three repeats of one setup
python3 score.py v2-full --flags                              # one run, with every unmatched flag
python3 score.py summary                                      # all runs in one table, writes results/
./rerun.sh 2>&1 | tee rerun.log                               # the full three-repeat rerun
```

Needs the Claude CLI signed in. Download the Guardrails pack from marketingprompthub.com/guardrails/ and set `GUARDRAILS_SKILLS` to its unzipped skills folder (`PAID_SKILLS` still works as the old name). The free Skills are at github.com/zaraonrecord/claude-marketing-skills.

`runs/*/_stale/` holds the first round 1 runs, kept for the record. They were made before the runner stored prompt hashes, so the rerun couldn't prove they used the same prompt and ran round 1 again instead. They aren't counted anywhere.

Each result stores a hash of the prompt, model and policy, and is reused only when that matches, so changing a Skill or the policy can't quietly mix old and new results. Each run also writes `runs/<version>/manifest.json`, which `score.py` reads to know which cases it used.

Tests: `uvx ruff check . && uv run --no-project --with pytest python -m pytest -q`
